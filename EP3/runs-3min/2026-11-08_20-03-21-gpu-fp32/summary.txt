EP1 question 3: pi with 14 decimals (error 1e-14)
  NEVER with FP32: FP32 keeps about 7 significant digits, so its needle test can never support 14 decimals. The numbers below are what it would take if the error kept falling.
  Needles needed: 5.6e+28 = (k / 1e-14)^2 (the free fit would say 6.0e+28: its slope is off by 0.001, stretched over 18 powers of ten)
  Speed of this GPU (all SMs): 1.09e+11 needles/s in the needle loop (7.04e+10 counting the serial part, at N = 3.2e+10)
  Time: 5.2e+17 s = 1.6e+10 years
  For N this large the serial part is nothing, so p -> 1 and time falls as 1/n (Amdahl):
  in 80 years you would need 2.1e+08 GPUs like this one (7.4e+09 SMs)
  N = 5.6e+28 does not fit in a 64-bit integer (max 9.2e18): it can only be extrapolated.
